<p><em>This issue on artificial intelligence and planning is 978 words long and it takes ~3.5 minutes to read. I hope you enjoy it.</em></p><p><strong>Static urban planning is dead.</strong> It ignores complexity, cherry-picks data, and generates urban fabric that cannot adapt. Even in governance systems with data-driven decision making, at best, the <em>context</em> is too narrow to cover the breadth of information needed to account for all the complexity of an urban system. <a href="https://thinkthinkthink.substack.com/p/cities-and-dna-september-2020">The solution is the </a><strong><a href="https://thinkthinkthink.substack.com/p/cities-and-dna-september-2020">living, adaptive city DNA</a></strong><a href="https://thinkthinkthink.substack.com/p/cities-and-dna-september-2020">.</a> DNA is not a single master plan; it is an optimized, conditional rule set that expresses differently across various tissues and contexts. The future of planning should be designed to encode this same logic, shifting urban strategy from static documents <em>to continuously tended, conditional rule sets that respond instantly to local signals and changing conditions. </em>This can now conceivably be built with heavy assistance from an LLM, spatial vector embeddings, a graph database and blockchain-based governance.</p><div><hr></div><h3>1. The Genotype: Deconstructing the City&#8217;s Logic</h3><p>First, we need a rigorous deconstruction of the city into its core primitives, translated into a computable representation. I previously called this the <a href="https://thinkthinkthink.substack.com/p/planning-as-a-platform-thinkthinkthink">Urban Knowledge Graph</a> but struggled to imagine how one could generate it. This is where LLMs provide a critical new capacity. They enable methodical <strong>synthetization of vast, unstructured knowledge</strong> - the &#8220;what&#8221; of the city buried in decades of research, policy papers, and case studies from across the globe.</p><p>The concept isn&#8217;t new; Lowry&#8217;s <em><a href="https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_memoranda/2006/RM4035.pdf">Model of Metropolis</a></em> (1964) and Forrester&#8217;s <em><a href="https://books.google.al/books/about/Urban_Dynamics.html?id=69JYAAAAMAAJ&amp;redir_esc=y">Urban Dynamics</a></em> (1969) were among the earliest attempts to capture the city as a formal system, each translating complex urban processes into structured, computable forms. Their models demonstrated that cities could be represented through interacting stocks, flows, and behavioral rules. Now, work from <strong>Batty, Bettencourt, and Bertaud</strong> confirms the city&#8217;s structure is governed by legible, decomposable primitives. The new addition are Large Language Models, which provide the missing piece to the puzzle.</p><p>Recent advances in data computation and ingestion now enable these LLMs to act as tireless analytical engines. They can extract key entities, relationships, and causal narratives from extensive corpora of information. More importantly, they can identify hidden patterns, non-linear correlations, and feedback loops that might elude conventional analysis.</p><p>When applied to urban research, these methods can surface the canonical variables that drive performance - from land markets and network connectivity to fiscal transfers and institutional constraints. The output can then be rendered as a complex system dynamics structure, with adaptive stocks, flows, and feedbacks that build on the lineage of Urban Dynamics.</p><div operative constructs: <em>floor area ratio</em>, <em>commuting time budgets</em>, <em>parcel-level viability, social added value </em>and tens of thousands of other variables that drive an urban system.</p></li><li><p><strong>Edges</strong> encode empirically grounded relationships - positive or balancing - with sensitivity ranges and relevant delays.</p></li></ul><p>This knowledge graph can ingest new evidence and recalibrate as new studies are indexed. Scenario questions then become structured interventions: <em>What happens if we reduce travel cost on a corridor?</em> The model returns directional effects with uncertainty bounds and, crucially, <strong>cites the evidence</strong> underpinning each link, ensuring auditability.</p><div><hr></div><h3>2. The Phenotype: Grounding Policy in Place</h3><p>The knowledge graph encodes the <em>rules</em>, but the <strong>geospatial embeddings</strong> determine the <em>context</em>&#8212;the soil in which the rules grow. A traditional map says &#8216;pavement.&#8217; An embedding says: &#8216;pavement, surrounded by 30-year-old oak trees, 15% annual humidity change, two schools, a bike lane and a 95th percentile noise level.&#8217; This gives us <strong>place-based priors</strong>. This enables the system to retrieve analogous urban fragments - places with similar signatures - to condition how the knowledge graph&#8217;s rule-set is expressed locally.</p><p>The second component provides the <strong>urban-geospatial context</strong>. Here, geospatial foundation models and globally consistent embeddings are the frontier. <a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaearth-foundations-helps-map-our-planet-in-unprecedented-detail/">Google Alpha Earth</a>, for example, provides 64-dimensional vectors at 10-meter resolution that summarize annual surface conditions. Merging this with <a href="https://overturemaps.org/">Overture Foundation</a>&#8217;s data could lead to a combination of top-down and bottom-up information. This is ideal for clustering, similarity search, and change detection. </p><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link image2 is-viewable-img" target="_blank" The Regulator: The future of governance</h3><p><a href="https://thesideview.co/journal/the-deeper-order-of-cities/">The core organizational engine for this paradigm is the </a><strong><a href="https://thesideview.co/journal/the-deeper-order-of-cities/">Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO)</a></strong><a href="https://thesideview.co/journal/the-deeper-order-of-cities/">.</a> DAOs are blockchain contracts that autonomously store and execute rules, using external interfaces (<strong>oracles</strong>) to sense the environment. This structure allows for a revolutionary, recursive form of local government, where individuals can belong to hundreds of interlocking community organizations at various administrative levels. This enables the implementation of a modern, continuously adapting <strong><a href="https://arl.human.cornell.edu/linked%20docs/Alexander_A_Pattern_Language.pdf">Pattern Language</a></strong>, where interactive blueprints automatically respond to neighboring developments. To ensure equitable and efficient collective decision-making - the political solvency of the DAO - the system can utilize <strong>Quadratic Voting (QV)</strong>. This method moves beyond a simple binary vote to measure the <strong>intensity of people&#8217;s preferences</strong> in collective decisions. Voters spend <strong>&#8220;voice credits&#8221;</strong> which convert to votes by their square root, meaning expressing stronger conviction incurs an escalating cost. This mechanism effectively normalizes the power of the largest stakeholders, greatly mitigating the <strong>tyranny-of-the-majority</strong> problem and providing a powerful, collaborative framework for localized, adaptive urban growth.</p><div><hr></div><h3>A New Planning Instrument</h3><p>Linking these components might yield the future of practical urban planning and growth. The <strong>Genotype</strong> encodes the universal logic. The <strong>Phenotype</strong> provides the local context. The <strong>Regulator</strong> channels the political will. A query (e.g., siting a new school) is no longer answered by committee or truncated data, but by traversing <strong>auditable causal pathways</strong>, conditioned on <strong>local signatures</strong>, and answered with quantified trade-offs and full citations. <strong>This is the shift from planning as a static blueprint to planning as a continuously updated, DNA-based operating system that finally allows the city to learn and adapt at the speed of life.</strong></p><div><hr></div><p>Did you like this issue of <a href="https://thinkthinkthink.substack.com/">thinkthinkthink</a>?

<p><em>This issue on communities is 467 words long and it takes ~2 minutes to read. It is written as part of a 7-day writing challenge with <a href="https://www.thetechprogressive.com/">The Tech Progressive</a>. Join the <a href="https://www.buildcities.com/">build_</a> Discord to join the discussion.</em></p><p>Twenty years ago I&#8217;d spin up my screeching 56 Kbit/s modem, open <a href="https://www.mirc.com/">mIRC</a> and connect to local servers with chatrooms upon chatrooms filled trivia, small-scale file-sharing, and deep conversations with internet acquaintances. I even met a couple of geeks for pizza in Tirana at the time; 15 to 20 year old&#8217;s <strong>dreaming about the digital frontier from the remnants of the iron curtain.</strong> It was the first time I felt like I belonged to a community of strangers with whom I shared some values, and could have complex conversations on fascinating topics.</p><p>Fifteen years ago I&#8217;d fire up World of Warcraft hop on my flying mount and, chat away with my fellow hardcore raiding guilders on TeamSpeak. It felt nothing like mIRC communities, but there was something there - something that was often more powerful and created stronger bonds than the mushrooming social media networks.</p><p>I had not felt a similar attraction to online communities until 2021. In the beginning there was a sudden <a href="https://www.joinclubhouse.com/">clubhouse</a> uptick back in February. Random and ephemeral voice chatrooms popped-up where one could have extremely interesting conversations on - for example - the technicalities of building cities on Mars. Most people I know have moved their clubhouse game to Twitter&#8217;s clone, spaces - but the concept remains the same. Have a long conversation about a well-defined topic with scores of people chiming in. These communities, especially when still running under the radar, have a very high signal-to-noise ratio. </p><div class="twitter-embed" data-attrs="{&quot;url&quot;:&quot;https://twitter.com/shl/status/1358961396432576513?s=20&quot;,&quot;full_text&quot;:&quot;Clubhouse is software eating cities.&quot;,&quot;username&quot;:&quot;shl&quot;,&quot;name&quot;:&quot;Sahil&quot;,&quot;profile_image_url&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;date&quot;:&quot;Tue Feb 09 02:10:19 +0000 2021&quot;,&quot;photos&quot;:[],&quot;quoted_tweet&quot;:{},&quot;reply_count&quot;:0,&quot;retweet_count&quot;:57,&quot;like_count&quot;:1083,&quot;impression_count&quot;:0,&quot;expanded_url&quot;:{},&quot;video_url&quot;:null,&quot;belowTheFold&quot;:false}" data-component-name="Twitter2ToDOM"></div><p>Some months later I <strong>discovered what might be the software infrastructure that will run the cities of tomorrow</strong>: the proliferation of web3 communities on Discord. While a lot of the current activity is concentrated on <a href="https://defipulse.com/">decentralized-finance</a> and <a href="https://www.constitutiondao.com/">constitution purveyors</a> - there&#8217;s a couple of growing digital islands that are re-imagining the urban dimension: <a href="https://discord.gg/twsHX3UGt5">build_</a>, <a href="https://discord.gg/DkF7t4YSZh">Praxis</a>, <a href="https://discord.gg/wBMb2rwFUV">cityDAO</a>, <a href="https://discord.gg/7uy7JvHPFc">1729ers</a> are only some of the highly active groups sharing amazing content, building social infrastructure, but most importantly creating highly specialized networks merging the gap between varied technical expertise and focused urban domain knowledge.</p><p>Most importantly I feel like for the first time the conversation on building and planning cities has expanded beyond urbanists and domain experts. <strong>In an interesting turn of events,</strong> <strong>I have yet to meet an architect or urban planner in my forays into this space. </strong>I&#8217;m sure there&#8217;s many fellow planners out there, but these communities certainly have zero resemblance to the professional homogeneity of <a href="https://isocarp.org/">ISOCARP</a> meetings. </p><p>The cities of tomorrow are being built by passionate individuals, whom are connecting and critically thinking - from first principles - about the urban fabric they want to live in. It&#8217;s <strong>public consultation in reverse</strong> - where the public is building cities, and the professionals might sometimes be asked for an opinion. Join these strange rationally discordant communities - you might mingle with the Mayors of tomorrow.</p><div><hr></div><p>Did you like this issue of&nbsp;<a href="https://thinkthinkthink.substack.com/">thinkthinkthink</a>?

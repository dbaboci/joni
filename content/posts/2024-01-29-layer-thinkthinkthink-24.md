<p><em>This issue on <a href="https://beta.getlayer.xyz">Layer</a> is 571 words long and it takes ~3 minutes to read. I hope you enjoy it.</em></p><p>A few years ago I embarked on an old aspiration to organize knowledge and data in better understanding and thinking about cities. I founded <a href="https://beta.getlayer.xyz">Layer</a>. After a decade in the public sector I realized that there is quite a bit of granular spatial data on cities available out there. While not necessarily decisive for most planning decisions and typically too low-resolution for developed western capitals, open spatial information is quite relevant to the world&#8217;s second- and third-tier cities.</p><p><strong>Layer at it&#8217;s first iteration attempts to do just that. Organize open and paid spatial datasets with global coverage under one easily accessible platform.</strong> Most datasets are either geospatially derived (with powerful and always improving satellite-based sensors from various space agencies as well as private sector actors) or crowdsourced (hats-off to <a href="https://www.openstreetmap.org/">OpenStreetMap </a>and <a href="https://www.worldpop.org/">WorldPop</a> for leading the way here). Most of our business revolves around custom service provision for a variety of clients including local government administration, real estate agencies, multilateral institutions, architecture and planning offices etc. (hint: <a href="https://beta.getlayer.xyz/contact/">contact us</a> or <a href="mailto:dbaboci@gmail.com">reach out to me directly</a> if that sounds interesting to you). The platform is <a href="https://beta.getlayer.xyz/signup/">free to sign-up</a> with <a href="https://beta.getlayer.xyz/catalogue">26 out of 53 layers</a> available for free for any city on
